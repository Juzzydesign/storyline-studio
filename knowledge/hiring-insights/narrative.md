## Positioning

Great work doesn't always speak for itself.

2026 Storyline Hiring Survey · live results.

Responses reflect individual perspectives, not official company positions.

## Portfolios still have a major influence on who gets interviewed

Your portfolio is not supporting material. For many hiring teams, it decides whether they speak to you at all.

## You have minutes to make your value clear

A reviewer does not need to understand everything immediately. They need to understand enough to want to keep looking.

## The thinking behind the work carries more weight

Strong visuals help, but hiring teams care more about how you think and decide.

Show strong work and the thinking behind it. A polished shell without clear decisions or contribution is not enough.

## Hiring teams want to understand what you did, and how you think

Lead with what you owned and how you decided. Metrics help, but they are not the first filter.

## Where portfolios lose hiring teams

Not every portfolio problem is about the quality of the work. Respondents also report problems with presentation, generic structures, unclear skills and insufficient context. Case studies can fail by being too long to scan or too thin on depth. A few also flag password walls and NDA-empty portfolios.

Make your skills and decisions easy to find. Keep case studies scannable and deep enough. Do not hide the work behind passwords or leave nothing visible after an NDA.

## AI is changing the questions hiring teams ask

Hiring teams want to see:

- Critical thinking — show why you chose one option over another
- AI fluency — show where AI helped, and where your judgment took over
- Authenticity — make your own decisions and contribution obvious

## Summary

- Portfolios still decide who gets a first interview.
- Most reviewers decide whether to keep looking in just a few minutes.
- Contribution and thinking beat generic polish.

The goal is simple: make the right evidence easy to find.
