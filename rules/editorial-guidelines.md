# Editorial guidelines

Storyline Design helps product designers and design systems designers communicate the value of their work through stronger positioning, portfolios and case studies.

Our approach is grounded in research, practical experience and insights from hiring teams.

We believe great work isn't enough if people can't understand why it matters.

Our goal isn't to teach designers how to make prettier portfolios or follow another framework. It's to help them understand what hiring teams are looking for, recognise the value in their existing experience and make informed decisions about how to present it.

**Core belief:** Your portfolio isn't a gallery. It's evidence for the role you want.

## Purpose

- Make hiring decisions more legible
- Show the thinking behind design work, not just the outputs
- Turn research and practice into repeatable content formats

## What we publish

- YouTube explainers and frameworks
- Newsletter notes
- Articles and blogs
- LinkedIn posts

## Editorial standards

1. Lead with the tension, not the process steps.
2. Prefer decisions and trade-offs over activity lists.
3. Titles should state the value, not hide behind vague labels.
4. Use concrete examples from real project moments.
5. Keep frameworks as tools, not scripts that flatten the story.
