# Research guidelines

## Purpose

Research should help me develop informed opinions, challenge assumptions and create useful content for designers.

Prioritise accuracy, context and original thinking over finding evidence that simply supports an existing argument.

## Research principles

- **Evidence over assumptions:** Don't present opinions or interpretations as established facts.
- **Challenge my thinking:** Look for contradictory evidence, not just findings that support my argument.
- **Prioritise original sources:** Use interviews, surveys, official documentation and primary research where possible.
- **Preserve context:** Consider sample size, methodology, date and limitations before drawing conclusions.
- **Avoid generalisations:** Findings from a small group of hiring teams shouldn't represent the entire design industry.
- **Stay curious:** Identify unanswered questions and opportunities for further research.
- **Names in knowledge vs content:** Real names are OK in `knowledge/` for storage and internal notes. Never use real participant names in outbound content (`content/`, posts, scripts, offers, or other public materials). When drafting content, describe people by role, segment, or anonymised label instead.

## What counts as research here

- Hiring manager and designer interviews
- Portfolio and case study reviews
- Patterns from your own hiring and teaching work
- Notes from design systems and product practice

## ICP interview dataset (storage only)

`knowledge/research/icp/` is an archive for interview notes and transcripts.

**Do not use it** for content drafts, positioning, offer ideas, automation, or AI-assisted writing unless explicitly asked. Prefer other `knowledge/` sources and `rules/` instead.

## Evaluating sources

Prioritise sources in this order:

1. Original Storyline research, interviews and documented experiences when directly relevant.
2. Primary sources such as research papers, official reports and documentation.
3. Credible secondary analysis with transparent sourcing.
4. Community discussions and personal experiences for qualitative perspectives.

Community opinions are useful for identifying patterns and questions, but shouldn't automatically be treated as representative evidence.

## Capture format

For each note in `knowledge/`, include:

1. **Date**
2. **Source** (person name OK in knowledge notes; link or context)
3. **Claim** in one sentence
4. **Evidence** (quote, example, or observation)
5. **Implication** for Storyline content
6. **Tags** (hiring, portfolio, decisions, titles, etc.)

## andling research findings

Always distinguish between:

- **Verified finding:** Directly supported by an identifiable source.
- **Personal observation:** Something I've experienced or noticed.
- **Interpretation:** A conclusion drawn from available evidence.
- **Hypothesis:** An idea that hasn't been sufficiently tested.

Never fabricate statistics, quotes, sources, interview responses or personal experiences.

Preserve exact quotes and numerical findings. Don't change their meaning to strengthen an argument.

## Storing new research

When adding information to the knowledge base, include:

- Descriptive title and topic
- Original source or file reference
- Date of publication or collection, if known
- Key findings
- Relevant quotes or data
- Context and limitations
- Personal interpretations, clearly separated
- Related topics or existing knowledge files

Prefer concise, structured Markdown files over lengthy AI-generated summaries.

Keep original source material accessible so findings can be checked later.

## Research for content

When helping with newsletters, LinkedIn posts, articles or YouTube videos:

- Start with the question or argument being explored.
- Find evidence both supporting and challenging it.
- Identify the strongest and most relevant findings.
- Suggest useful examples rather than overwhelming the content with statistics.
- Flag claims that need verification.
- Explain uncertainty when the evidence is limited.

Research should strengthen the author's thinking, not replace it.

## AI behaviour

- Don't automatically agree with my conclusions.
- Don't invent information to fill research gaps.
- Don't present AI-generated summaries as original sources.
- Don't silently change or reinterpret research findings.
- Don't conduct extensive external research when existing knowledge is sufficient.
- Ask before storing sensitive, confidential or unpublished third-party information.
- Real names may appear in `knowledge/`; never include them when drafting outbound content.
- Reference the source file or URL when presenting findings.

**Priority: Accurate, contextual research that helps me form my own perspective.**

## Using research in content

1. Start from a claim in `knowledge/`
2. Draft in `content/`
3. Check voice against `rules/writing-style.md`
4. Check framing against `rules/editorial-guidelines.md`
